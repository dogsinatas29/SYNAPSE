# v0.3.34.22 Criticality Ontology

The Criticality layer (Phase 6) distinguishes between **Authority** (nodes that dominate execution and references) and **Criticality** (nodes that are structurally essential to the domain). 

## The Core Distinction
> Authority ≠ Criticality

- A `Logger` has massive Authority (high fan-in) but zero Criticality (deleting it breaks nothing architectural).
- A `DomainSchema` has low Authority (few direct callers) but absolute Criticality (deleting it invalidates the entire data pipeline).

## Findings Ontology

### 1. `CORE`
Nodes that define the fundamental domain, state, or primary pipelines of the system.
- **Criteria:** Participates in critical Data/Control pipelines, acts as a State/Policy owner, or is a structural bridge (Boundary Crosser).
- **Impact:** Removal causes catastrophic pipeline or state collapse.

### 2. `SUPPORTING`
Nodes that facilitate or optimize core behavior but do not define it. 
- **Criteria:** Mid-tier authority, localized to a specific boundary, does not own global state.
- **Impact:** Removal causes localized feature degradation.

### 3. `UTILITY`
Nodes that provide generic, highly reused functionality completely agnostic to the domain.
- **Criteria:** Extremely high fan-in, zero outgoing dependencies to `CORE` nodes, isolated in Boundary Islands.
- **Impact:** Removal causes compile errors but no architectural logic shifts.

## Analysis Pipeline

Instead of running new raw graph algorithms, the `CriticalityAnalyzer` synthesizes Phase 1-5 outputs:

```text
Findings (Role, Authority, Flow, Boundary)
  ↓
CriticalityAnalyzer
  ↓
CriticalityEvidence
  ↓
CriticalityRule
  ↓
Findings (CORE, SUPPORTING, UTILITY)
```
